The Tools Got Good. Most Business Owners Haven't Noticed.

A few years ago, a decent small-business website took a designer, a developer and someone to manage them. For a simple site, one person directing Claude can now do the job.

Think about the owner of a plumbing company, a bakery or a dental office. They're busy running a business. Most haven't seen what these tools do now, and they aren't going to find the time to learn.

That gap is the opportunity. Someone has to ask the questions, write the brief, check the result and put the site live. You can be that person.

I'm not going to promise you a number. I'll show you the process I used, and you can judge it.

“Why Would Anyone Pay Me When They Could Use AI Themselves?”

They could. Plenty of people could also fix their own sink.

A business owner can open Claude. They still have to work out what to ask for, gather their photos and details, review every page, connect the domain and check that the contact form works. They don't have time for that. You take on the project, and those jobs become yours.

You ask the questions. You check the site on a phone. You make the changes you agreed to. You hand it over so the owner never has to think about it again.

The course teaches you to do that with AI, and to recognize the jobs that are too big for you yet. You start with simple sites.

A Building Session, Start To Finish

  1. Open your agency folder in Claude and say "Start my session." Claude tells you where you are and the next step.
  2. If it's a new lesson, read it in Eden, or ask Claude for the short version. Then you do it together.
  3. Claude shows you three design directions with previews. You pick one, then build the site section by section.
  4. Check every page at desktop and phone size. Ask for changes. Approve it before it goes online.
  5. Say "wrap." Claude saves your work, updates your dashboard and tells you what's next.

You make the decisions, talk to clients and approve the work. The tools do the typing.

Sites That Don't Look Like AI Made Them

You've seen it: the same gradient, the same layout, the same vague headline. Clients notice too.

So before Claude builds anything, it proposes three design directions with previews. You choose one. The builder kit also includes an anti-generic-design checklist, which gives you specific things to look for and fix before you show the site to anyone.

A suggested pace is about one hour a day for 30 days. Your own pace will vary.

This Is For You If You're Willing To Make The Calls

You'll fit if you're comfortable on a computer, you're willing to learn by building, and you can check an AI's work before it goes to a client.

You don't need a design degree or any coding experience. You do need to pick up the phone, send the emails and follow through on what you agree to. Some people will say no. Some won't answer. The course gives you scripts and a follow-up system. You still have to start the conversations.

Questions

Do I need to know how to code?

No. Claude writes the code. You learn to describe what you want, check the result and ask for changes.

Does "One Hour" mean I can build a site in an hour?

No. It's the name of the course. An hour a day is a pace that makes room for the work. Setup, revisions and client projects take as long as they take.

Will I get clients?

I can't promise that, and neither can anyone else. The course gives you a process: pick a niche, find businesses, reach out, follow up and track every attempt. You control how many conversations you start.

I use Claude, but never to build things. Is this for me?

Yes. If you can chat with Claude, you have what you need to begin. The course adds the building and the business, and Claude coaches you through both.

What if the sites look obviously AI-generated?

You pick from three design directions with previews before anything gets built, and the kit includes a checklist for catching generic design. You'll learn what to look for.

What if a client asks for something I can't build?

Claude helps you decide whether to scope it down, bring in a partner or refer it. Bigger builds like online shops and members' areas take more skill, and the course shows you where that line is.

Will I hit Claude's usage limits?

You might. Claude Pro has a limit that resets every five hours and a weekly one. The course works on Sonnet by default and keeps each session to one task. If you reach a limit, nothing is lost, and the message tells you when it resets.

What's the Eden chat for, if Claude does the coaching?

Setup help before Claude is working, and quick questions when you're away from your computer.

Is the contract and invoice material legal advice?

No. It's general information and templates. Have a professional review anything you rely on.

Are the prices in the course what I should charge?

They're examples for working through the lessons. They aren't typical results or a promise of what clients will pay.
