# Pricing

What to charge for a static site. The single hardest part of freelancing for most people, and the place where new freelancers leave the most money on the table.

---

## When to read this

User says:
- "How much should I charge?"
- "What's my rate?"
- "The client asked for a number"
- "Am I underpricing?"

---

## The two pricing mindsets

### Cost-based (what most people do)

> "It'll take me 40 hours. My rate is $50/hour. So it's $2,000."

This is how plumbers and accountants think. It's the wrong way for design/build work. Reasons:

- It punishes you for being fast. Get faster → earn less.
- It treats your time as the product. Your **outcome** is the product.
- It anchors low. Beginners assume their hours are worth what entry-level salaries pay.
- It encourages padding ("better say 60 hours to be safe").

### Value-based (what experienced freelancers do)

> "This site will generate $50K/year in leads for them. The going rate for that kind of marketing-impact site is $5K–$10K. I'll quote $6K."

You're not selling hours. You're selling a working website that the client uses to make money or save time. Price it relative to the **value to them**, not the **cost to you**.

Value-based pricing is uncomfortable at first. Practice it.

## Project minimums

Below a floor, projects are not worth doing. Below $1,000 is almost always a regret. Below $500 is *always* a regret.

| Project type | Reasonable minimum |
|---|---|
| Single landing page | $750–$1,500 |
| 3–5 page small marketing site | $2,500–$5,000 |
| 5–10 page site with Stripe + form | $4,000–$8,000 |
| Same + light CMS overlay | $5,000–$10,000 |
| Anything beyond ~10 pages | quote individually, but rarely below $8K |

These ranges are for a freelancer with **0–2 years of paid client experience**, charging in USD, in a US/UK/Canada/AU market. Adjust by purchasing power for other markets. Adjust up after each project (see "the ratchet" below).

If a client's budget is below your floor: **don't lower your price**. Lower the scope. Drop a page, drop the CMS, drop the custom photo direction — but keep your number above the floor.

## Package shapes

Three packages, low-medium-high, beats one number every time. Two reasons:

1. **Anchoring** — the high-end number makes the middle one feel reasonable.
2. **Decision shift** — the client moves from "yes / no to your price" to "which one fits."

Example for a small-business site:

```
Essential  — $3,500
  • 3 pages (home, about, contact)
  • Contact form
  • Mobile responsive
  • SEO basics
  • 2 revision rounds

Recommended  — $5,800  ⭐
  • Everything in Essential
  • 5 pages
  • Newsletter signup + MailerLite integration
  • Light CMS for non-technical editing
  • Lighthouse + accessibility pass
  • 3 revision rounds
  • 1-month post-launch support

Comprehensive  — $9,500
  • Everything in Recommended
  • Up to 10 pages
  • Stripe checkout integration
  • Custom photography direction
  • Animation + interaction polish
  • 30 days of edits included
  • 3 months of support
```

Star the middle one. Most clients will pick the starred option — they don't want to look cheap (Essential) or extravagant (Comprehensive).

## Deposit conventions

| Project size | Typical deposit |
|---|---|
| Under $2K | 100% upfront, no installments |
| $2K–$8K | 50% deposit, 50% on launch |
| $8K–$15K | 50% deposit, 25% midpoint, 25% on launch |
| $15K+ | 33% deposit, 33% midpoint, 33% on launch, possibly more milestones |

The deposit is non-refundable (see `03_contracts.md`). The midpoint payment is gated on a deliverable (design approval, beta link, etc.) — name it specifically in the contract.

## The ratchet

After every completed project, **raise your prices.** Even a little. Reasons:

- You're better than you were last project (and your prices should reflect that).
- The work is the same as last time; charging the same fee is a real-terms pay cut against inflation.
- You'll keep underpricing forever if you wait until "you feel ready."

Rule of thumb: **+10–25% per project** until clients start pushing back. When pushback starts (you lose ~30–50% of proposals), you've found your market price. Hold there for a while.

If clients **never** push back on price, you're underpriced.

---

## Discounts (when and how)

Discounts are dangerous. They anchor your value lower and set permanent expectations. Acceptable cases:

- **Cause-based**: real nonprofits, mission-aligned work you'd take partly pro-bono anyway. Discount up to ~30%.
- **Reciprocal**: you genuinely want this client in your portfolio for credibility. Make the discount explicit and one-time: "I'm offering $X off my normal rate because I'd love to add you as a case study."
- **Volume**: discount the *second* project, not the first. "If you bring me another site after this, I'll do it for $Y."

Never discounts for:
- "Family friend"
- "It'll be a good portfolio piece for you" (your portfolio is your problem, not theirs)
- "Exposure"
- Generic "tight budget" — that's a scope issue, not a price issue

## Talking about money on the call

When asked "what do you charge?" without context:

```
"It depends on scope, but for the kind of site you're describing,
most of my projects land between $4K and $8K. Once I see your scope,
I can give you a firm number."
```

Three things this does:
1. Gives a range so you're not the one who quoted first.
2. Ties the answer to scope (creates the path to the proposal).
3. Doesn't lock you in.

When the client says their budget is below your range:

```
"That budget is going to constrain scope. Here's what I could do at
that price: <list a tighter version>. If you want the full version
we discussed, we'd need to land closer to $X."
```

Lower scope, not price.

## Common pitfalls

- ❌ Charging hourly when you should charge by project. You're not selling hours.
- ❌ Quoting on the call. Sleep on every number.
- ❌ Stretching your floor downward "just this once."
- ❌ Pricing in your own currency for an overseas client at their currency's purchasing power. Either price in their currency (and adjust the floor) or in your currency (and stand on it).
- ❌ Never raising prices. The ratchet only works if you actually ratchet.
- ❌ Underestimating revisions. A "small marketing site" with 4 stakeholders is **not** small.
